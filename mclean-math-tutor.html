02 · Fit

For students who need stronger foundations—or a higher ceiling.

This is specialized upper-elementary math instruction, not a general homework-completion service.

A strong fit

Your child needs focused math instruction.

  • Fractions, decimals, multiplication, division, or number sense are shaky
  • Grades look fine, but understanding is thinner than the report card suggests
  • Your child can follow a procedure but struggles to explain why it works
  • Your child is capable and needs more demanding mathematical work

Probably not the right fit

The main goal is getting tonight’s worksheet finished.

  • Drop-in homework completion is the only need
  • You are looking primarily for last-minute test cramming
  • Your child needs services outside Matthew’s instructional scope

Serving McLean families with a deliberately small, founder-led model.

Schools our students have attended

Students we’ve served have attended Franklin Sherman, The Potomac School, and Longfellow, among other Northern Virginia public and private schools. School names describe student experience only; Algebra Ready is not claiming school affiliation or endorsement.

Why in-home tutoring?

The in-home model gives McLean families a consistent teacher without adding another tutoring-center trip to the week. Matthew brings the assessment, instructional plan, and materials to the student.

What parents receive

A clear starting point, direct instruction from the same educator each week, and periodic checks that show whether the targeted skill is improving.

What makes Algebra Ready different?

The service is intentionally narrow: Grades 4–6, mathematics, one student at a time, taught directly by the founder. That means less handoff, less generic curriculum, and more accountability for the instructional result.

Common questions

What McLean parents usually want to know.

What grades do you tutor?

Algebra Ready specializes in Grades 4–6, where number sense, fluency, fraction understanding, decimals, and reasoning become increasingly important for later algebra success.

Do you help with homework?

Homework can provide useful evidence, but it is not the product. Sessions target the underlying skill or misconception that will matter beyond one assignment.

What if my child already gets good grades?

That can still be a strong fit. Some students need extension rather than remediation. The assessment looks for depth, flexibility, and readiness—not only whether a child is passing class.
