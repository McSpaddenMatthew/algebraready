# First-Cohort Case Study System

This system turns the first 5–10 completed Algebra Ready Sprints into
publishable evidence without inventing quotes, overstating results, or exposing
student information.

The website must not publish a case study until every item in the publication
gate below is complete.

## Non-negotiables

- Invite every family in the first cohort to participate. Do not collect only
  likely success stories.
- Use a private student ID such as `AR-001`. Never commit a student's name,
  school, neighborhood, contact information, or identifiable work to this
  public repository.
- Record baseline evidence before the first instructional session.
- Use the same skill definition and either the same items or a documented
  parallel form for reassessment.
- Report raw counts with small assessments (for example, `2 of 6` to `5 of 6`),
  not inflated-looking percentages.
- A quotation must be the family's exact words. Light edits for spelling or
  length require the family's approval of the final wording.
- Do not claim that Algebra Ready caused a grade, test-score, placement, or
  advancement outcome.
- Keep attendance and practice completion with the evidence so a reader can
  understand the conditions under which the result occurred.
- A family may approve evidence without approving a quote, photograph, name, or
  other identifying detail. Default to anonymous publication.

## Cohort workflow

### 1. Enroll and assign an ID

Assign `AR-001` through `AR-010` in enrollment order. Keep the crosswalk
between IDs and family names outside this repository in a private operational
record.

Tell the family that Algebra Ready measures progress for instructional
purposes. Ask about public case-study participation separately; enrollment
must not depend on publicity consent.

### 2. Capture the baseline before instruction

Record:

- grade at the time of the Sprint;
- the parent's stated goal in their own words;
- the diagnostic date and form/version;
- raw results by domain and number of items possible;
- the highest-leverage instructional priority;
- one de-identified work sample or exact student explanation;
- the behavior the student currently demonstrates independently;
- the conditions of administration, including prompts or supports used.

Store original assessments and student work securely outside the public
repository.

### 3. Preserve evidence during the Sprint

After each session, record:

- attendance;
- the skill and representation taught;
- one piece of independent evidence;
- the next instructional decision;
- whether assigned practice was completed.

At Week 4, record the instructional review and any change to the learning
sequence. Ask the parent one neutral question:

> What, if anything, have you noticed changing in how your child approaches
> mathematics?

Preserve the answer verbatim. "Nothing yet" is valid data.

### 4. Reassess after Week 8

Record:

- reassessment date and form/version;
- raw results by the same domains used at baseline;
- a comparable work sample or student explanation;
- whether the student demonstrated the target independently;
- attendance out of eight sessions;
- practice completion out of assigned opportunities;
- the written 90-day recommendation.

Do not substitute a classroom grade, MAP score, or parent impression for the
Sprint's baseline-to-reassessment comparison. Those may be reported only as
separate context with their source and date.

### 5. Interview and draft

Ask the parent:

1. What made you seek a readiness review?
2. What did the initial assessment clarify?
3. What specific change, if any, did you observe?
4. What can your child now do more independently?
5. What would you tell a parent considering Algebra Ready?

Use the family's answer exactly when quoting it. Draft the case study with
`PUBLIC_CASE_STUDY_TEMPLATE.md`.

### 6. Obtain written approval

Send the family the complete draft exactly as it would appear. Obtain explicit
written approval for:

- the anonymized student description;
- the baseline and reassessment evidence;
- any work sample;
- every quotation;
- any optional name, image, school, or location detail.

Save the approval date and approved version in the private operational record.
Do not place the parent's email or signature in this repository.

### 7. Publish without cherry-picking

Publish each completed, approved case that passes the gate. A credible case
study does not need a dramatic score increase. Clarifying a hidden gap,
demonstrating a specific new behavior, or making a responsible next-step
recommendation can all be useful outcomes.

## Publication gate

| Requirement | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Baseline | Collected before instruction with form/version and raw counts |
| Reassessment | Comparable form and conditions documented |
| Instruction | Priority and material instructional changes documented |
| Participation | Attendance and practice completion reported |
| Claim | Limited to what the evidence directly supports |
| Quote | Verbatim and approved, or omitted |
| Privacy | No identifying information unless separately approved |
| Approval | Family approved the complete final draft in writing |
| Disclaimer | Individual-outcomes disclaimer included |

If any row fails, do not publish the case study.

## Minimum website case-study format

Each public case study should include:

1. **Student context:** grade only, unless the family approves more.
2. **Starting priority:** the precise concept or reasoning behavior.
3. **Baseline evidence:** raw result plus a short description of what the
   student demonstrated independently.
4. **Instructional response:** what Algebra Ready prioritized and why.
5. **Eight-week evidence:** comparable raw result and observable behavior.
6. **Participation context:** sessions attended and practice completion.
7. **Next plan:** the written 90-day recommendation in one sentence.
8. **Parent voice:** one approved verbatim quote, if available.
9. **Evidence note:** assessment form/version, dates generalized to month/year,
   and the individual-outcomes disclaimer.

## Launch target

Collect complete evidence for all of the first 10 Sprint students. Publish the
first five cases that finish the Sprint, pass the publication gate, and receive
family approval. Continue until 10 approved cases exist; do not delay the
website waiting for all 10.
