06 · Readiness & fit

Built for one kind of family.

A narrower fit creates better groups, stronger instruction, and a clearer result.

A strong fit

Capable. Successful. Not fully secure.

  • Usually earns B’s or A’s but may not be working at full potential
  • Knows procedures but struggles to explain, defend, or transfer them
  • Has a focused gap in fractions, number sense, or multi-step reasoning
  • Can attend weekly and complete 30 minutes of practice

Not the right fit

Clear boundaries protect results.

  • You primarily need homework completion, drop-in help, or test cramming
  • Your child needs broad or intensive intervention beyond a small-group setting
  • Weekly attendance or short independent practice is not realistic right now

08 · Common questions

Clear answers before the first call.

Does my child need to be struggling?

No. The Academy is designed primarily for capable students who earn acceptable or strong grades but may rely on memorized procedures, avoid unfamiliar problems, or have one focused foundation gap. Students needing broad, intensive intervention are usually better served elsewhere.

Will twelve weeks make my child completely Algebra-ready?

Algebra readiness is built over time, so we do not promise to complete the entire upper-elementary sequence in one term. The initial twelve weeks target the one priority most likely to improve future readiness, measure it against a written standard, and end with a clear recommendation to graduate or continue.

What happens after the first session?

You receive an annotated before-and-after example showing what your child attempted, what instruction changed, and what the next practice will target. It is evidence of the first movement, not a claim that the entire skill has been mastered in one hour.

What exactly does Academy enrollment include?

The initial term includes a written baseline and progress standard, a personal twelve-week plan, twelve 60-minute sessions in a group of four or fewer, three 10-minute practices each week, a first-session evidence sample, weekly parent updates, formal reviews at Weeks 4, 8, and 12, a catch-up plan, final reassessment, and a written next step.

How can semi-private instruction still be personal?

Students are matched around a shared mathematical domain, but each enters at the point shown by the assessment. The group creates discussion and energy; the roadmap, prompts, and next steps remain individual.

Who leads my child’s academic plan?

Matthew leads the diagnostic standard, designs each student’s learning sequence, and reviews the evidence. Weekly sessions follow that plan and are taught by Matthew or a carefully selected Algebra Ready educator trained to the same standard.

When do I learn the schedule and tuition?

Groups meet in person in the Charleston area. Current location, day, time, and Academy tuition are shared during the parent call before the student assessment is scheduled. Students are matched by readiness, grade, and schedule.

What if my child does the work but misses the goal?

If your child meets the published attendance and practice requirements, completes the final reassessment, and does not meet the written progress standard agreed before Week 1, we revise the plan and provide one additional four-week Academy cycle at no tuition. Grades, test scores, and course placement are not guaranteed.

What happens after the initial 12 weeks?

We compare the baseline with the final reassessment. Your child either graduates with a written next-step plan or receives a focused recommendation to continue in four-week Academy evidence cycles.
