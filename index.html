03 / Readiness & fit

The right structure for the right student.

Selectivity protects the quality of the group. The readiness review is used to determine whether the Sprint matches the student’s needs, goals, and working habits.

Algebra Ready may be a strong fit if your child:

  • Earns acceptable grades but is not consistently challenged
  • Knows procedures but struggles to explain why they work
  • Has hidden gaps in fractions, number sense, or multi-step reasoning
  • Is preparing for more advanced middle-school mathematics
  • Responds well to high expectations and deliberate practice

It may not be the right fit if:

  • You are looking primarily for homework completion or drop-in tutoring
  • Your child needs intensive intervention beyond a small-group setting
  • Consistent attendance and between-session practice are not possible

What deeper understanding looks like in real life.

Four anonymized snapshots based on responses from families of students Matthew has taught. Student names, schools, and other identifying details have been removed.

01
Featured alumni outcome snapshot · Strong grades, hidden gap

An A was only the starting point.

She earned strong grades, but familiar answers masked a lack of conceptual depth. When a problem changed form or became more demanding, she was more likely to disengage than reason her way through it.

Starting point

Good grades with short, surface-level explanations.

Priority

Explain relationships, persist through unfamiliar questions, and reason beyond a memorized procedure.

Transfer

At a grocery store, she independently compared 3/4 and 2/3 and worked through the reasoning beside the cart.

It is worth it—especially if you have a child who is doing fine, but you want to push them to see their potential. Suzanne M. · Parent of a former student
Parent testimonial · Engagement

When shaky foundations looked like disinterest.

His parent initially saw a child who simply did not care about math. The assessment suggested that uncertainty in foundational concepts was making it difficult for him to engage. As those foundations strengthened, she saw him begin to want to attend math class and develop a lasting appreciation for the subject.

Continuing priority Strengthening fraction-to-decimal conversions.
“If you want your child to do more than say, ‘Two is the answer’—to explain why it is two and whether it could be anything else—I would really consider Algebra Ready.” Emma · Parent
Parent outcome snapshot · Place value

Strong potential, but an important idea was missing.

She knew that one million was greater than one hundred thousand, but she could not clearly explain why. The work strengthened the relationships among place value, fractions, and decimals so she had more than a procedure to rely on when questions became less familiar.

Parent-reported context Her parent later reported strong performance on MAP and a practice college-readiness assessment. No tutoring-caused score gain is claimed.
“It’s worth it. Do it. I’d do it ten times over.” Susie · Parent
Parent outcome snapshot · Higher-order reasoning

At the top of his class—but not yet at his ceiling.

He earned strong grades but was often disengaged. The assessment identified higher-order reasoning as the next opportunity. His parent then saw him begin attacking demanding questions instead of pushing them aside.

Real-world transfer While waiting in line, he spontaneously connected 1/2, 50/100, and 500/1,000 as equivalent representations.
