Case 01 · Fifth grade

He was earning strong grades—and an assessment revealed a specific place to grow.

20-question checks 10 → 18 Approximately three weeks
Classroom performance
High B’s and low A’s; successful on state testing
Priority identified
Connecting place value, decimals, and fractions
Instructional goal
Replace surface procedures with flexible reasoning

The starting point

Robbie completed homework independently, earned strong grades, and performed well on state assessments. He rarely needed help and generally described school as “fine.”

His parents were not worried that he was failing. They were curious whether he was being fully challenged and prepared for what came next. His teacher had also noticed signs of under-engagement, including rushed work and inconsistent attention to detail despite strong performance.

What the assessment revealed

Robbie answered 10 of 20 questions correctly. He knew enough procedures to succeed in class, but the relationships among place value, decimals, and fractions were not yet flexible enough for consistent application.

He did not need generic homework help or a complete reteaching of fifth-grade mathematics. He needed the right high-leverage concept made visible and then extended.

The instructional response

Instruction repeatedly returned to a place-value chart to connect multiplication and division by powers of ten, movement between whole numbers and decimals, and equivalent forms such as 1/2, 0.5, and 50/100.

The goal was to make the structure clear enough that one insight would improve performance across several problem types—not simply add more routine practice.

Evidence and next step

After approximately three weeks, Robbie answered 18 of 20 questions correctly on a later check, compared with 10 of 20 initially.

Instruction then moved into deeper fraction work while continuing to revisit place-value relationships. His family continued because they valued consistent challenge beyond basic correctness.

Case 02 · Sixth grade

A hardworking student rebuilt the unfinished skills making math harder than it needed to be.

20-question checks 6 → 18 Approximately six months
Classroom performance
Approximately 70% on tests and 75–85% on report cards
Priorities identified
Fluency, place value, fractions, and number sense
Instructional goal
Stabilize the foundations with the greatest downstream effect

The starting point

Ava was attentive, cooperative, and willing to work. Those habits helped her remain reasonably successful in class, but familiar calculations still required more time and effort than her parents expected.

Multi-digit multiplication was not automatic. Being allowed to use a calculator felt like relief rather than simply a convenience. Her parents saw that partially learned skills were placing an unnecessary load on every new topic.

What the assessment revealed

Ava answered 6 of 20 questions correctly. Her work showed real effort and reasonable thinking; she was not guessing. Several foundational ideas were simply incomplete.

For example, when multiplying 568 by 10, she shifted the number by two place values instead of one. Similar weaknesses appeared in fraction understanding and general number sense.

The instructional response

Instruction first stabilized addition and subtraction accuracy, multiplication and division fluency, place-value relationships, and fraction meaning and operations.

Ava benefited from more repetitions than some students need. The expectation did not change; the amount of spaced, successful practice increased until the learning became durable.

Evidence and next step

After approximately six months, Ava answered 18 of 20 questions correctly on a later assessment, compared with 6 of 20 initially. She also became substantially more successful with fraction problems.

The work then shifted from repairing old gaps to advance preparation. Earlier exposure gave her enough runway to reach mastery without lowering the standard.

Case 03 · Fourth grade

Her parents wanted to close the gaps before middle school. One year later, the trajectory looked different.

Initial diagnostic
Approximately 2 of 20 correct
Fraction baseline
1 of 8 correct
Priorities identified
Fraction magnitude, place value, and independence

The starting point

Autumn enjoyed reading and performed strongly in literacy, but mathematics told a different story. Her math grades, teacher feedback, state assessment results, and MAP performance had left her parents concerned that the gaps would compound as she approached middle school.

Their goal was direct: rebuild the foundation while there was still time to change the trajectory.

What the assessment revealed

Autumn answered approximately 2 of 20 questions correctly, including only 1 of 8 fraction problems. She often did not know how to begin an unfamiliar problem.

Although several skills needed attention, fraction magnitude and the place-value system stood out as the two highest-leverage priorities.

The instructional response

Autumn used fraction manipulatives to build and compare quantities. She repeatedly located fractions on a large physical number line, while place-value models made changes through multiplication, division, and equivalent representations visible.

The objective was not merely to get answers right. It was to help her see where numbers belonged, explain why relationships were true, and start problems without waiting for an adult.

Evidence and next step

Over the following school year, Autumn’s MAP math percentile rose from approximately the 45th to approximately the 88th. Her mother also reported meaningful gains in confidence and independence at school and at home.

Autumn continued working with Matthew for three years. The purpose shifted from intervention to continuity: preserve the progress, strengthen new skills, and prepare for increasingly advanced mathematics.
